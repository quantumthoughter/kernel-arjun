## 1. Root texts
- *Paramādibuddhatantra* — the original, attributed to Shambhala king Sucandra; no longer extant.
- *Laghu-kālacakratantra-rāja* ("Sovereign Abridged Kālacakra") — the Kālacakratantra we have,
  attributed to Shambhala king Mañjuśrīyaśas.
- *Vimalaprabhā* ("Stainless Light") by Puṇḍarīka — the authoritative commentary.
- *Sekkodeśa* — surviving fragment of the original, commented on by Nāropa.
- Dating (modern Buddhist studies): Śrī Kālacakra + Vimalaprabhā completed **1025–1040 CE**.

## 2. The three Kālacakras (the master key)
1. **Outer (bāhya)** — the cosmos / world system / time cycles of planets and elements.
2. **Inner (adhyātma)** — the individual body; subtle body of channels, winds, drops.
3. **Alternative (anyatra)** — the mandala of deities; the enlightened cosmos as palace.
All three correspond by the principle **yathā bahye tathā dehe** ("as it is outside, so it is within the body").
Nondual, mutually pervasive; macrocosm mirrors the individual. Cosmology used as a
*heuristic/contemplative model* to train nondual perception.

## 3. The five chapters of the Kālacakratantra
1. **Outer Kālacakra** — cosmology (Vaibhāṣika, Samkhya, Purāṇas, Jain sources);
   the Kālacakra calendar; birth and death of universes; Sun and planets; five elements;
   the **myth and prophecy of Shambhala**. World emerges from emptiness and the force of time.
2. **Inner Kālacakra** — human gestation and birth; functions of the body;
   the **subtle body**: channels (nāḍī), winds/breaths (prāṇa/vāyu), drops (bindu).
   The potentials (bindus) that give rise to states, and processes flowing from them.
3. **Initiation / preparation** — mainly the *abhiseka* of Kālacakra; requirements for meditation.
4. **Sādhana and yoga** — meditation on the **mandala and its deities** (generation stage),
   and the **Six Yogas of the Completion Stage** (inner heat, luminosity, dream, etc.).
5. **Jñāna (gnosis)** — the result/fruit: immutable bliss; union of emptiness and compassion;
   Buddhahood as Kālacakra (the *Ādibuddha*, Supreme Primordial Buddha); the four Buddha bodies
   (Sahajakāya, Dharmakāya, Saṃbhogakāya, Nirmāṇakāya). Enlightened awareness is innately present
   in every being, needing only purification of the winds to be recognized.

Time & cycles: *kāla* = time = gnosis; *chakra* = wheel = the knowable. Outer cycles = days/months/
years/zodiac; inner cycles = prāṇa circulating in the nāḍī wheel; both linked to the 12 links of
dependent origination and 12 zodiac signs.

## 4. Lineages
- **Jonang** — Dölpopa Shérap Gyeltsen (~1292–1361); *shentong* ("other-emptiness"); strong Kālacakra emphasis.
- **Gelug** — the 14th Dalai Lama's school; most famous modern expositor.
- **Sakya** — the **Ra lineage** (Ra Lotsawa).
- **Kagyu** — the **Dro lineage** (Drolo Sherap Drak).
- **Nyingma** — also practices Kālacakra.
- Origination legends: taught by Śākyamuni Buddha at Dharanikota (Amaravathi, Andhra Pradesh)
  while bilocating at Gridhrakūṭa; passed to Shambhala; returned to India 966 CE by Chilupa /
  Kālacakrapada the Greater; standard two-lineage (Ra "rva lugs" / Dro "bro lugs") framing.

## 5. Iconography
- Kālacakra (deity, male) in *yab-yum* with **Viśvamatā** (female); multi-headed, multi-armed
  (4 faces / 24 arms typically); standing on symbols of the winds.
- **Kālacakra mandala** — a palace floor-plan mandala; cosmos as the deity's palace.

## 6. Dalai Lama teaching format (the public arc)
The 14th Dalai Lama, Tenzin Gyatso, gives public Kālacakra initiations worldwide.
Recurring teaching arc:
**outer Kālacakra → inner Kālacakra → alternative (mandala) → generation stage → completion stage → jñāna.**
(Largest historic events were at Bodh Gaya; these are the most prominent modern transmissions.)

## 7. The union of bliss and emptiness (editorial decision — corrected)
The tantra's completion stage works with sexual yoga (karmamudrā), the *yab-yum* of
Kālacakra and Viśvamatā, and the use of bliss as a path — because bliss is held to be a
"facsimile of the realization of emptiness." This is not a footnote; it is the **union of
wisdom (prajñā/emptiness) and method (upāya/compassion)**, which the tantra states is its
essential teaching and its goal (jñāna = "the mind of immutable bliss").
**Decision: INCLUDE it as part of the map** — described with dignity, scholarship, and restraint,
because the book must not lie about the tradition it maps. It is also *our* central metaphor:
the union of intelligence and compassion; "love is the engine." Exclude only gratuitous
explicit depiction (taste, not censorship). Canon terms: yab-yum, Viśvamatā, karmamudrā,
mahāmudrā, bliss-and-emptiness.
