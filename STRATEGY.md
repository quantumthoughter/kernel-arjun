# Kernel-Arjun — Strategy

*Written 2026-09-29. The compass for every decision after this.*

---

## The honest premise

**The code is not the moat.** A capable AI can reproduce a durable agent kernel
in a weekend. We must not pretend otherwise — and must not build a strategy that
depends on the code being secret.

What *cannot* be copied quickly:

- **The proof.** A real 91,269-word book, real numbers, a real run. Others must
  earn that claim; we already hold it.
- **The name.** Becoming "the durable-execution kernel" in the market's head.
- **The trust.** Budgets-as-law, independent verification, an auditable ledger —
  verification *as reputation*.
- **The distribution.** Being found first; the standard people default to.
- **The taste.** Knowing which problems matter (verified long-form, sovereign
  automation, regulated workflows).
- **The running service.** Operational reliability others won't build themselves.

**Therefore: open the reproducible part (the code). Own the non-reproducible
part (brand, proof, trust, service).**

---

## Decision: Open Core, MIT kernel

- **The kernel is MIT.** Maximum adoption, matches the four vows, and MIT is how
  infrastructure becomes a standard (Kubernetes, Redis, Terraform did this).
- **No license walls.** No AGPL, no source-available, no dual-license at the
  kernel. Friction kills the standard-setting play.
- **The moat is services and verticals, not licenses.**

### What is open (the funnel)
- The engine: ledger, loop, Council, verifiers, budgets, resume.
- The SDK: `from arjun import Arjun`.
- The MCP server, the dashboard, the CLI.
- The benchmark/methodology (the capacity ladder).

### What stays ours (the business, built later, optionally closed)
- **Kernel-Arjun Cloud** — hosted multi-day runs, dashboards, cost governance.
- **Enterprise** — SSO, audit export, SLAs, private deployment, custom verifiers.
- **Vertical kits** — *Verified Long-Form* (books/reports), *Sovereign
  Automation* for India.
- **Support & certification** — training, the "Kernel-Arjun engineer" credential.

---

## Why openness wins here specifically

As models grow more capable and autonomous, the scarce resource shifts from
**"can the model do it?"** to **"can the system be trusted to run it — for days,
at bounded cost, provably?"** That is governance. Whoever names and defines that
layer owns something everyone needs. **Open is how you win a standard; services
are how you get paid for it.**

---

## The standing principle

> **Open the kernel. Keep the roadmap.**
> Every new capability is asked one question first: *is this the reproducible
> part (open it) or the operating/trust part (keep it)?*

Most things are the reproducible part. That is fine. The brand, the proof, and
the hosted reliability are the business.

---

## Low-regret moves (do now)

1. MIT + public GitHub. (irreversible-ish, but low risk at this stage)
2. Claim the namespaces: PyPI `kernel-arjun`, GitHub org, domain.
3. Keep enterprise/cloud ideas *named but unbuilt* — decide later.
4. Publish the proof (the book, the numbers) as the flag.

## Deliberately NOT doing (yet)

- Not shipping a hosted service before the standard is set.
- Not license-gating anything.
- Not optimizing for revenue before adoption.
- Not over-building features nobody asked for.
